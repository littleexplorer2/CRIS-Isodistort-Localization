Sets of Distortions. We obtain distortions belonging to a particular isotropy subgroup
by applying the vector⃗η to the basis vectors of the subspaces of S. For example, the
distortions associated with the isotropy subgroup P4mm belonging to Γ−
4 are obtained
by applying⃗η = (a, 0, 0) to the three basis vectors of every subspace of S generated
by Γ−
4 . In the case of (a, 0, 0), these would be the ﬁrst basis vector in each subspace:
{u1x = a1}, {u2x = a2}, {u3x = a3}, {u4x = u5x = a4}, where a1, a2, a3, a4 are
independent arbitrary constants. Any one of these four distortions can lower the
Pm¯3m symmetry of BaTiO3 to P4mm.
As another example, consider⃗η = (a, a, b). This represents a 2-dimensional invariant
subspace of representation space. The isotropy subgroup symmetry determined by this⃗η
is the monoclinic space group Cm. Applying⃗η to the basis vectors of the subspaces of S,
we obtain the following 8 distortions belonging to Cm: {u1x = u1y = a1}, {u1z = b1},
{u2x = u2y = a2}, {u2z = b2}, (3) {u3x = u4y = a3}, {u5z = b3},

independent arbitrary constants. In order to lower the Pm¯3m symmetry of BaTiO3
to Cm, however, at least one of a1, a2, a3, a4 and one of b1, b2, b3, b4 must be nonzero.
Phase transitions and order parameters. Consider a phase transition in BaTiO3
from the cubic Pm¯3m structure to the monoclinic Cm structure discussed above. The
primary order parameter is (a, a, b), belonging to the IR Γ−
4 . There is only one secondary
order parameter which produces atomic displacements: (0, a, ¯a), belonging to the IR Γ−
5 .
This order parameter generates the isotropy subgroup Amm2, which is a supergroup
of Cm. There is only one distortion belonging to Amm2:
{u5x = −u4x = −u3y = u5y = a}. In the phase transition, the 8 distortions associated
with the primary order parameter and the distortion associated with the secondary order
parameter will all appear. There are 9 independent distortions in all, equal to the
number of free atomic position variables in the Cm structure.
Coupled order parameters. Suppose we couple the order parameter (a, 0, 0) belonging
to Γ−
4 with the order parameter (0, a, 0) belonging to Γ−
5 . This means that we
superimpose the distortions associated with those two order parameters. The order
parameter (a, 0, 0) belonging to Γ−
4 lowers the symmetry to P4mm, and the order
parameter (0, a, 0) belonging to Γ−
5 lowers the symmetry to P¯4m2. Together, they lower
the symmetry to Pmm2, which is a subgroup of both P4mm and P¯4m2. A phase
transition from the Pm¯3m structure to this Pmm2 structure can only happen if the
coupling between the two order parameters is strong enough so that both order
parameters become nonzero simultaneously.
